Sericulture Foliar Disease Guide

Clinical identification, biological cycles, chemical treatments, and safe harvesting intervals for *Morus alba*.

🌿 Healthy Mulberry Foliage (Disease Free)

Normal morphology of nutritious silkworm feed (*Morus alba / Morus indica*)

Safe for Feeding

Key Visual Characteristics:

  • Uniform deep green leaf blade without chlorotic (yellow) halos or discoloration.
  • Intact venation network without brown pustules or fungal mycelium.
  • Smooth, succulent texture optimal for silkworm (*Bombyx mori*) instar consumption.

Maintenance Routine:

Apply balanced NPK fertilizer (300:120:120 kg/ha/yr for irrigated gardens) split across cropping cycles. Maintain proper inter-row weed clearance and mulch to preserve soil microbial vitality.

Sericulture Feed Value:

High moisture content (70–75%), rich in crude protein (20–24%), providing complete nutritional foundation for high shell ratio cocoon production.

⚠️ Mulberry Leaf Rust (*Cerotelium fici*)

Fungal foliar pathogen prevalent during humid post-monsoon periods (Sep – Jan)

Fungicide Required

Visual Symptoms:

  • Starts on the lower surface of mature leaves as tiny, pinpoint yellowish-brown spots.
  • Rapidly erupts into raised, circular, reddish-brown to orange powdery pustules (uredinia).
  • Severely affected leaves dry up, curl inward, turn leathery, and defoliate prematurely.

Agronomic & Chemical Management:

  • Sanitation: Clip and burn infected leaves early before spores spread via air currents.
  • Canopy Pruning: Thin dense branches to improve airflow and direct sunlight inside the canopy.
  • Chemical Spray: Spray Mancozeb (Dithane M-45) 0.2% (2g/L water) or Copper Oxychloride 0.2% on both leaf surfaces.
🐛 Silkworm Safety Warning:

Safe Waiting Period: 10 to 14 days! Never feed freshly sprayed leaves to silkworms. Chemical residue causes flacherie and worm mortality.

🚨 Mulberry Leaf Spot (*Pseudocercospora mori / Phloeospora*)

Destructive necrotic pathogen flourishing under high humidity and warm temperatures

High Severity

Visual Symptoms:

  • Circular to irregular dark brown, black, or reddish-brown necrotic spots across leaf blade.
  • Spots enlarge, coalesce into large dead patches, often bordered by a distinct chlorotic halo.
  • Dead tissue within old lesions frequently drops out, creating a "shot-hole" appearance.

Agronomic & Chemical Management:

  • Debris Cleanup: Gather and incinerate all fallen diseased foliage to break the spore inoculum cycle.
  • Irrigation Practice: Avoid overhead sprinklers; water at soil level to prevent water splashes spreading fungal conidia.
  • Fungicide Application: Spray Bavistin (Carbendazim 50% WP) 0.1% (1g/L water) or Chlorothalonil 0.2%.
🐛 Silkworm Safety Warning:

Safe Waiting Period: 10 to 12 days after Bavistin spray. Thoroughly wash harvested leaves in clean water and dry in shade before rearing feed.

❓ Unknown / Unidentified Condition (OOD)

Out-of-distribution patterns, novel viruses, bacterial blights, or physiological stress

Expert Inspection

When the AI diagnostic engine detects low confidence (< 70%) or overlapping symptoms, the sample is safely flagged as Unknown / Unidentified Disease rather than forcing a hazardous misclassification.

Recommended Investigative Actions:
  1. Rule out Mulberry Mosaic Virus (yellow mosaic mottling and leaf puckering).
  2. Rule out Bacterial Blight (Pseudomonas syringae) characterized by water-soaked black lesions.
  3. Rule out Powdery Mildew (Phyllactinia corylea) with white powdery patches on lower leaf surface.
  4. Submit leaf samples to the Central Sericultural Research & Training Institute (CSR&TI) extension centre.
